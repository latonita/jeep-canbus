Jeep Carputer Screens

True 240×320 for the Waveshare ESP32-S3 panel. In swipe order first — welcome, then the five peers, DRIVE being where the boot settles and SETTINGS last — then the boards that are not on the swipe axis: the reset dialog, what TYRES looks like today, and three that are designed but not built. Zoom to judge legibility; these are life size.

HORIZONTAL AXIS - five peers in order, hard ends, no wrapping: DRIVE, TRIP, TYRES, HEALTH, SETTINGS. This is the only axis a moving driver uses: a thumb flick anywhere on the panel, no aiming.

One swipe moves at most ONE page, however hard it is thrown. A scroller left to itself predicts where the momentum would carry it and snaps to whatever is nearest, which skips two cards on a brisk flick - fine in a photo gallery, wrong here.

DRIVE is FIRST and is where the boot settles. An earlier order put it second, so that it was the one card reachable from either direction in one swipe, with TRIP one flick left. That is a real property and it was given up deliberately: the card the driver wants most often should cost ZERO flicks, not one from each side, and a hard end is its own kind of landmark - you cannot overshoot off the front of the list. TRIP moves to one flick right, which is where the second-most-wanted card belongs.

SETTINGS is last on purpose, and now costs four flicks rather than three: it is the only page read with the truck stopped, so it should be the most expensive and is never landed on by accident.

There is NO idle timeout. An earlier draft returned to DRIVE after 30s; it was removed. The driver put the screen on TYRES deliberately, looked at the road, and found a different page waiting. A page changes only when a finger changes it.

The vertical axis (drill-downs) is designed but NOT BUILT. The settings long-press is gone - settings is a page on this axis now.

Landscape

LANDSCAPE - 320x240, the same five peers

Rotation is a SETTINGS row, not a second product. Same pages, same order, same one-flick-one-page rule, same hard ends, same absence of an idle timeout. What changes is which axis is scarce: portrait has 320px of height to spend and only 240 of width, landscape has exactly the reverse, and every difference in this band follows from that one fact.

THE DOT RAIL MOVES INTO THE STATUS BAR. Portrait can afford a 26px rail of its own along the bottom - that is 8 percent of 320. Landscape cannot: the same 26px is 11 percent of 240, taken off the only axis that is short. The bar is 320px wide and carries three short items, so the dots ride in its true centre and the whole bottom rail goes back to content. Landscape gets 218px of content height rather than 192. The rail is the one piece of chrome that does not survive the rotation unchanged, and it is worth the discontinuity.

TWO COLUMNS REPLACE A STACK. Portrait puts the hero number on top and the supporting grid beneath it. Landscape puts the hero in a 187px left band and the same values in a 112px right stack, divided by one rule. Those two numbers are not free to move independently: content = band + stack is (320 - 1 - 2*PAD) - PAD, which is 279px at a 10px inset and 271 at 12, whatever the divider does. It was 189/130 at 12px insets, and the stack came down to 112 only once the hero units left the header and wanted room on the numeral's own line - TRIP needs 187 for 247.8 plus KM, the stack needs 92 for 4163 rpm, and 279 covers both where 271 does not. Nothing is dropped, nothing is added, nothing is reordered - it is the same instrument turned ninety degrees, which is what makes a runtime toggle safe rather than a second design to keep in sync.

THREE BOARDS DO NOT TAKE THE TWO-COLUMN TREATMENT, and each refused it for a measured reason rather than a taste. TYRES is already a grid and gets a wider one. FAULTS is a list and gets wider rows. SETTINGS was drawn in two columns first and the label would not fit - see its note; that is the one place the rotation made something harder rather than easier.

NO NEW NUMERALS. Every numeral on every landscape board is 66px or 30px CSS - font_digits_64 and font_digits_30 - and the landscape set generated no new digit face. That was enforced, not lucky: four tyre pressures at hero size want 2x73px of digits plus labels plus chrome, which is 238 of the 218px available, so hero-size tyres were drawn and then thrown out.

THE RIGHT-HAND STACK ROW is one 48px block - a 16px caption line box and a 35px value line box, the value hanging off the CAPTION'S BASELINE at +13 rather than off the bottom of its line box at +16 - centred in whatever height the row is given, because a stack of four 53s and one 56 has to look evenly set. It was 51 in a 53px row, which left the digits 1px off the hairline under them: font_digits_30's base_line is 0, so unlike every other face here its baseline IS the bottom of its line box and it has no descender space to absorb the difference. An all-caps caption never uses font_label_14's 3px of descender, so taking it back costs nothing visible and spends it on the gap that was wrong - 2px above the caption, 3 below the digits. The unit sits on the VALUE'S baseline (+35) too, not 10px above it, which is the same rule the hero units now follow; the stack and the hero used to read as two typographic systems.

THE Y COORDINATES ON THESE BOARDS ARE THE FIRMWARE'S. THE INK IS NOT. The boards are CSS Barlow Condensed and Barlow's figures are about 0.727em, so font-size:66px draws 48px digits where font_digits_64 draws 64. Every block boundary here is the shipped number - header 5..21, hero line box 24..97, sub-line 108..124, a stack value 15..50 against its rule at 53 - but the glyph inside the hero's 73px band is 16px shorter than the panel's, so it sits high in the band and the 11px sub-line gap reads wider here than it does on glass. Take the y's off these boards. Do not take the optical gaps, and never take a width.

ONE HERO BLOCK, SHARED BY DRIVE, TRIP AND HEALTH. These three cards had each grown their own hero geometry and ended up at three different heights: the header at y=8 / 5 / 4, the numeral at 24 / 76 / 52, the sub-line at 103 / 155 / none. Flicking between them the whole card jumped, which reads as three designs rather than as one instrument with three cards. They now sit on one set of numbers, and the numbers are DERIVED from the two faces' metrics rather than chosen, which is what stops them drifting again. font_digits_64 is line_height 73 with base_line 1, so its baseline is 72px down and its glyphs span y+9 to y+73; font_label_14 is 16/3, so its own baseline is 13px down. Header at 5, in both bands. HERO_Y = 24. UNIT_Y = HERO_Y + 72 - 13 = 83, which puts a label-face unit's baseline exactly ON the numeral's. SUB_Y = HERO_Y + 73 + 11 = 108. DRIVE's third acceleration figure is SUB_Y + 18 = 126 - one 16px line and 2px of air under the sub-line, derived from it rather than typed, because typing it is what left it overlapping when the sub-line went 103 to 108.

THE 11 IN SUB_Y IS THE ONE FIGURE HERE THAT IS NOT DERIVED. It was 6, which is portrait's gap, and 6 is too tight in this band: font_digits_64's glyphs reach y+73, the very bottom of its line box, so the measured gap and the SEEN gap are the same number, where a face with descender space to spare would have looked airier at the same figure. It was set by eye against the panel, and it is the only number on this block that was.

THE HERO'S UNIT CAME BACK DOWN ONTO ITS BASELINE, and the boards lost their font_label_20 keys with it. DRIVE and HEALTH used to park the unit in the header as a 20px key, which was the safe place for it: at 20px an inline L/100 starts where DRIVE's hero ends. TRIP had always drawn KM on the hero's baseline instead, and that is the arrangement that won - right-aligned to the left column's content edge at x=197 so it holds still while the number's width changes underneath it, in font_label_14 (11px on these boards), not font_label_20. That face still exists and is still right for a unit key genuinely parked in a header, which is what ACCEL's S is; a hero's unit belongs to its number.

IT COULD NOT SIMPLY MOVE THERE, and DRIVE is where that bill came due. L/100 in font_label_14 is 35px right-aligned at x=197, so its left edge is 162; the hero starts at x=8; and font_digits_64 renders 100.0 in 159px. So DRIVE's hero DROPS ITS DECIMAL at three integer digits - 100, not 100.0 - which brings it to about 109 and keeps the two apart, and at 100 L/100km the tenth is noise. A board drawing 100.0 in that hero would be showing something the firmware can no longer produce. These boards show 9.4, which is the ordinary case; the rule is the reason the unit could leave the header at all. HEALTH is safe by accident - V is 8px - and TRIP's KM is 18.6.

NO NUMBER LIES ABOUT ITS AGE

ATMA delivers only about 61 of 168 frames/sec, so gaps are normal. Every value HOLDS its last reading and goes DIM once past its TTL, rather than blinking to dashes on a gap that is entirely routine. Values that were never available, or that a module answered with its all-ones sentinel, show dashes instead - a frozen-but-plausible number is the failure mode that gets believed.
